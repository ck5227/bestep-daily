閱讀 Reading

1. The scholarship committee will ______ each application based on grades and volunteer experience.

  1. evaluate
  2. evacuate
  3. elevate
  4. eliminate
看答案與詳解

正解 A　evaluate「評估」，委員會依成績與志工經驗評估申請，選 (A)。evacuate 撤離、elevate 提升、eliminate 淘汰，語意不合。

2. Because the speaker's flight was delayed, the seminar was ______ until next Thursday.

  1. preserved
  2. prevented
  3. postponed
  4. proposed
看答案與詳解

正解 C　be postponed until...「延期到…」，選 (C)。preserve 保存、prevent 預防、propose 提議，都不能與 until 下週四搭配。

3. Please keep your receipt as ______ of payment in case there is a problem with your registration.

  1. prove
  2. proof
  3. proven
  4. approval
看答案與詳解

正解 B　空格在 as 之後、of 之前需要名詞，proof of payment「付款證明」，選 (B)。prove 是動詞、proven 是過去分詞；approval「核准」語意不合。

4. The professor asked us to ______ the main ideas of the article in no more than two hundred words.

  1. summon
  2. assume
  3. consume
  4. summarize
看答案與詳解

正解 D　summarize「摘要」，用兩百字內摘要文章重點，選 (D)。summon 召喚、assume 假設、consume 消耗，語意不合。

5. Dormitory residents are ______ for keeping the shared kitchen clean.

  1. respectful
  2. responsible
  3. responsive
  4. reasonable
看答案與詳解

正解 B　be responsible for...「負責…」，選 (B)。respectful 有禮貌的、responsive 反應快的、reasonable 合理的，都不接 for + V-ing 表負責。

6. The new study rooms are ______ to all students, but they must be reserved at least one day in advance.

  1. available
  2. valuable
  3. avoidable
  4. variable
看答案與詳解

正解 A　be available to...「可供…使用」，選 (A)。valuable 有價值的、avoidable 可避免的、variable 多變的，語意不合。

7. To be ______ for the exchange program, applicants must have a GPA of at least 3.0.

  1. visible
  2. flexible
  3. edible
  4. eligible
看答案與詳解

正解 D　be eligible for...「有資格參加…」，選 (D)。visible 可見的、flexible 有彈性的、edible 可食用的，都是字尾相似的干擾。

8. The lab assistant gave us clear ______ on how to use the microscope safely.

  1. constructions
  2. inspections
  3. instructions
  4. introductions
看答案與詳解

正解 C　instructions on how to...「如何…的指示」，選 (C)。construction 建造、inspection 檢查、introduction 介紹，語意不合。

9. Due to the small room size, club membership is ______ to fifty students per semester.

  1. limited
  2. listed
  3. linked
  4. located
看答案與詳解

正解 A　be limited to + 數量「限於…」，選 (A)。listed 列出、linked 連結、located 位於，都不能表示人數上限。

10. Many students find it hard to ______ their time between part-time jobs and schoolwork.

  1. blame
  2. balance
  3. borrow
  4. bother
看答案與詳解

正解 B　balance A between B and C「在 B 與 C 之間平衡時間」，選 (B)。blame 責怪、borrow 借、bother 打擾，語意不合。

11. The university ______ a new policy that bans smoking anywhere on campus.

  1. admitted
  2. adapted
  3. adjusted
  4. adopted
看答案與詳解

正解 D　adopt a policy「採行政策」，選 (D)。adapt 改編／適應 與 adopt 拼字相近是主要陷阱；admit 承認、adjust 調整也不合。

12. If you have any questions about the assignment, feel free to ______ me by email.

  1. concern
  2. contain
  3. contact
  4. contract
看答案與詳解

正解 C　contact me by email「用 email 聯絡我」，選 (C)。concern 涉及、contain 包含、contract 簽約，字形相近但語意不合。

Debate Club Finds Success by Listening More

When Chen Wei-lun joined the university debate club two years ago, the team had not won a single national competition in a decade. This October, the club took first place at the National University Debate Championship, beating 31 other teams.

According to Chen, now the club's captain, the turning point came when the team changed how it trained. "We used to spend most of our practice time writing speeches," he said. "Now we spend half our time listening to the other side's arguments and summarizing them before we respond." The club also began inviting students from other departments, such as law and engineering, to act as practice opponents.

Coach Lisa Fang believes the new method made team members calmer during competitions. "Good debaters don't just speak well. They notice what the other side has missed," she explained. She also pointed out that the club's membership has grown from 12 to 35 students since last year.

The team will represent the university at the Asian Debate Open in Singapore next March. Because travel costs are high, the club is asking for donations and will hold a public practice debate on December 5, with all ticket money going to the travel fund.

Sharing a Room with Someone from Across the World

When I found out my new roommate was from Brazil, I was excited but also nervous. My English was fine for class, but I wasn't sure I could live with someone who did everything differently.

The first few weeks were not easy. Lucas liked to cook late at night and play music while studying, while I preferred silence. We had a few small arguments, mostly about noise. Then our resident advisor suggested we write a "room agreement" together. We decided on quiet hours from 11 p.m. to 7 a.m. and agreed that whoever cooked would clean the kitchen the same night.

Once the rules were clear, things got much better. Lucas taught me to make a Brazilian dish called feijoada, and I took him to the night market to try stinky tofu. He hated it, but he still talks about it. By the end of the semester, my spoken English had improved more than in three years of classes, simply because we talked every day.

My advice for anyone with an international roommate: don't wait for problems to get bigger. Talk early, write things down, and be curious. You may end up with a friend for life, as I did.

35. What does the speaker recommend doing when you need help in a crowd?

  1. Shout as loudly as possible
  2. Call the police first
  3. Wait for someone to notice
  4. Ask one particular person
看答案與詳解

正解 D　講者建議在人群中求助時，指著某一個人直接請他幫忙，選 (D)。(C) 等人注意正是旁觀者效應會造成的問題。

聽力原文

In today's class, we'll look at something psychologists call the bystander effect. It describes a surprising pattern: the more people who see someone in trouble, the less likely any one of them is to help. In a famous series of experiments in the nineteen sixties, students were asked to sit in a room and fill out a form. Then smoke began to enter the room. When students were alone, most of them left to report the smoke within a few minutes. But when they sat with two other people who ignored the smoke, only about one in ten reported it. Why does this happen? One reason is that responsibility is shared. Each person thinks someone else will act. Another reason is that we look at others to decide what is normal. If nobody else seems worried, we assume there is no emergency. The good news is that simply knowing about the bystander effect makes people more likely to help. So if you ever need help in a crowd, point to one specific person and ask them directly.
