閱讀 Reading

1. The seminar room can ______ up to forty people, so we don't need a bigger venue.

  1. accommodate
  2. accompany
  3. accomplish
  4. accumulate
看答案與詳解

正解 A　accommodate 表示「容納」，句意為研討室最多可容納四十人。(B) accompany 陪伴；(C) accomplish 完成；(D) accumulate 累積，都只是字首相同。

2. Attendance at the orientation is ______ for all new exchange students; no one may skip it.

  1. voluntary
  2. temporary
  3. mandatory
  4. ordinary
看答案與詳解

正解 C　後半句說「任何人都不得缺席」，可知是「強制的」mandatory。(A) voluntary 自願的，意思正好相反；(B) temporary 暫時的；(D) ordinary 普通的。

3. Keep your receipts, and the department will ______ you for your travel expenses after the conference.

  1. remind
  2. reimburse
  3. replace
  4. reinforce
看答案與詳解

正解 B　保留收據是為了事後「核銷、償還」費用，reimburse sb. for sth. 是固定用法。(A) remind 提醒；(C) replace 取代；(D) reinforce 加強。

4. Graduating seniors are given ______ when registering for courses, so they can choose before other students.

  1. publicity
  2. majority
  3. minority
  4. priority
看答案與詳解

正解 D　「可以比其他學生先選」表示享有「優先權」priority。(A) publicity 宣傳；(B) majority 多數；(C) minority 少數。

5. These are only ______ results; the final report will be published after more data are collected.

  1. preliminary
  2. previous
  3. primitive
  4. precious
看答案與詳解

正解 A　與後面的 final report 對比，這裡是「初步的」結果，選 preliminary。(B) previous 先前的，無法與 only 及後文的對比搭配；(C) primitive 原始的；(D) precious 珍貴的。

6. Before choosing your thesis topic, you should ______ your advisor.

  1. consume
  2. consult
  3. conclude
  4. confuse
看答案與詳解

正解 B　選論文題目前應該「請教」指導教授，consult 後面直接接人。(A) consume 消耗；(C) conclude 下結論；(D) confuse 使困惑。

7. The elevator is ______ out of service, but it should be working again by Friday.

  1. permanently
  2. originally
  3. increasingly
  4. temporarily
看答案與詳解

正解 D　後半句說週五前會恢復運作，所以是「暫時」停用，選 temporarily。(A) permanently 永久地，與後文矛盾；(B) originally 原本；(C) increasingly 越來越。

8. Ms. Lin had to ______ the invitation to speak at the forum because she would be abroad that week.

  1. decrease
  2. dedicate
  3. decline
  4. decorate
看答案與詳解

正解 C　因為那週人在國外，只好「婉拒」邀請，decline an invitation 是常見搭配。(A) decrease 減少；(B) dedicate 奉獻；(D) decorate 裝飾。

9. The walk from the dormitory to the science building takes ______ ten minutes.

  1. appropriately
  2. approximately
  3. apparently
  4. absolutely
看答案與詳解

正解 B　數字前用 approximately 表示「大約」。(A) appropriately 適當地，拼字相近是主要干擾；(C) apparently 顯然；(D) absolutely 絕對地。

10. Your student card is ______ for four years and must be renewed if you stay longer.

  1. valid
  2. vivid
  3. vague
  4. vacant
看答案與詳解

正解 A　證件在四年內「有效」用 valid，後文的 renewed（換發）也是線索。(B) vivid 生動的；(C) vague 模糊的；(D) vacant 空著的。

11. The professor asked us to ______ our essays by adding more examples and fixing grammar mistakes.

  1. reverse
  2. reveal
  3. revive
  4. revise
看答案與詳解

正解 D　增加例子、修正文法就是「修改」文章，選 revise。(A) reverse 顛倒；(B) reveal 揭露；(C) revive 使復甦，都是 rev- 開頭的形似字。

12. Because of low ______, the Tuesday evening yoga class has been canceled.

  1. employment
  2. entertainment
  3. enrollment
  4. environment
看答案與詳解

正解 C　課程取消的原因是「報名人數」太少，low enrollment 是固定說法。(A) employment 就業；(B) entertainment 娛樂；(D) environment 環境。

Therapy Dogs Help Students Relax before Finals

For one week every December, the second floor of the Student Center at Lakeside University sounds more like a park than a study space. Ten trained therapy dogs and their owners visit campus as part of "Paws and Relax," a program started three years ago by the university counseling center.

The idea came from counselor Rita Kuo, who noticed that many students were unwilling to visit the counseling center even when they were under great stress. "Some students think that asking for help means they are weak," she says. "But nobody feels embarrassed about playing with a dog." While students are waiting in line or petting the animals, counselors chat with them casually and hand out information about sleep, time management, and the center's services.

The results have been encouraging. In a survey of last year's 800 visitors, 92 percent said they felt calmer after a fifteen-minute session. More importantly, the number of first-time visits to the counseling center in the following month rose by a third.

The program has rules to protect the animals as well. Each dog works no more than two hours a day, and groups are limited to six students at a time. Students must wash their hands before entering and may not feed the dogs. "The dogs are volunteers, too," Kuo says with a smile. "We want them to enjoy it as much as the students do."

This year, because of high demand, students need to book a time online. Walk-in visitors will be admitted only if there are empty places.

What a Semester Off Taught Me

At the end of my second year, I did something that shocked my parents: I applied to suspend my studies for one semester. I wasn't failing. In fact, my grades were fine. The problem was that I had chosen accounting because it seemed safe, and I could no longer explain to myself why I was studying it.

My parents worried that I would fall behind my classmates, and to be honest, so did I. To persuade them, I wrote a plan with three goals: work full-time for three months, pay my own living costs, and talk to at least ten people about their jobs.

I found a position at a small company that exports bicycle parts. I expected to be bored. Instead, I discovered that the numbers I had studied in class told stories. A late payment from a customer in Europe meant that we could not order materials, which meant that an entire shipment was delayed. For the first time, accounting felt like solving a puzzle rather than memorizing rules.

The interviews were just as valuable. A nurse, a baker, and a software engineer all told me the same thing in different words: no job is interesting every day, and what matters is whether you care about the problem you are solving.

I returned to school in February, one semester behind my friends, but with a clear reason for being there. I also added courses in international trade. Taking time off is not right for everyone, and it does cost time and money. But if you feel lost, stepping away for a while may be the fastest way forward.

聽力原文

Today's topic is light pollution, which means too much artificial light at night. You might think that more light simply makes cities safer and more convenient, but researchers have found some serious costs. First, consider wildlife. Baby sea turtles find the ocean by moving toward the brightest horizon, which used to be the moon over the water. Now, hotel and street lights lead many of them in the wrong direction. Migrating birds are also confused by bright buildings and often crash into them. Second, there is human health. Our bodies produce a hormone called melatonin in darkness, and it helps us sleep. Bright light at night, especially blue light from screens and L E D lamps, reduces this hormone, so people sleep less deeply. Finally, there is wasted energy. One study estimated that about thirty percent of outdoor lighting in the United States shines up into the sky, where nobody needs it. The good news is that this is one of the easiest kinds of pollution to fix. Covers that point light downward, warmer coloured bulbs, and motion sensors can all make a difference without making streets less safe.
