1. Because of the heavy rain, the outdoor graduation ceremony has been ______ for next Sunday.

  1. reminded
  2. refreshed
  3. reserved
  4. rescheduled
看答案與詳解

正解 D　句意：因大雨，戶外畢業典禮「改期」到下週日。reschedule 改期。(A) 提醒、(B) 使清新、(C) 預訂，都不符合「改到另一天」的意思。

2. The library will ______ its opening hours during final exams, staying open until 2 a.m.

  1. extend
  2. expand
  3. expect
  4. explore
看答案與詳解

正解 A　延長時間用 extend，後面「開到凌晨兩點」說明時間變長。(B) expand 多指空間或規模擴大；(C) 期待；(D) 探索。

3. All participants must wear a name tag so that staff can easily ______ them.

  1. imply
  2. inspire
  3. identify
  4. impress
看答案與詳解

正解 C　戴名牌是為了讓工作人員「辨認」參加者，identify。(A) 暗示、(B) 激勵、(D) 使印象深刻，與名牌的功能無關。

4. Your essay should be well ______, with a clear introduction, body, and conclusion.

  1. organic
  2. organized
  3. ordinary
  4. original
看答案與詳解

正解 B　有清楚的引言、本文、結論，表示文章「組織良好」well organized。(A) 有機的；(C) 普通的；(D) 原創的，雖可形容文章但與後面的結構說明不搭。

  1. apply

正解 A　apply for 申請，搭配 permit（許可證）。(B) appeal 上訴、(C) approve 核准（是學校做的事）、(D) appear 出現。

6. The professor gave us a ______ list of readings, but we only need to read the first three articles before the midterm.

  1. narrow
  2. brief

  4. lengthy

Phone-Free Classrooms: One Department's Experiment

Last spring, the Department of Foreign Languages tried an unusual experiment. In ten of its classes, students were asked to place their phones in a box at the front of the room at the start of each lesson. They could collect them during the break or in an emergency.

At first, many students were unhappy. "I felt nervous without my phone, especially in the first few weeks," said Allen Kuo, a second-year student. Some students also worried that they would miss important messages from their part-time employers.

By the end of the semester, however, opinions had changed. In a survey of 280 students, 64 percent said they paid more attention in class, and more than half said they took better notes. Teachers reported that discussions became more active, as students were more willing to speak up.

The results were not all positive. Average test scores in the phone-free classes rose only slightly, by about two points, compared with other classes. Professor Linda Yeh, who led the experiment, says this is not surprising. "Grades depend on many things. What we really wanted to know was whether students would feel more engaged, and they clearly did."

The department plans to continue the policy next year, but with one change: students will be allowed to keep their phones if they need them for class activities, such as looking up words in an online dictionary.

What a Mountain Village Taught Me

When I signed up for the winter teaching program, I thought I was going to help children with their English. I didn't expect that two weeks in a small mountain village in Hualien would teach me more than any course I had taken.

Our team of six university students taught at an elementary school with only forty pupils. We had planned our lessons carefully, using slides and online videos. On the first day, however, we discovered that the school's internet connection was too slow to play anything. We had to throw away most of our plans.

That night, we sat together and rebuilt the lessons using what we had: paper, colored pens, and a box of old picture cards we found in the school library. To our surprise, the children loved the games we invented. One shy girl, who had not said a word on the first day, ended up leading her group in a spelling race.

Back on campus, I noticed that I had changed as a student, too. I used to panic when things did not go as planned, like when a group member dropped out of a project. Now I see these moments as a chance to try something new. I have also stopped depending so much on technology to study; sometimes a pen and paper help me think more clearly.

If you are thinking about joining the program next year, my advice is simple: prepare well, but be ready to let your plans go.
